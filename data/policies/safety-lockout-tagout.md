# SOP-SAF-07 — Lockout-Tagout (LOTO) Procedure

## 1. Purpose

To prevent injury from the unexpected energisation, start-up or release
of stored energy in hydraulic test rigs, presses and machine tools during
servicing and maintenance.

## 2. Application

This procedure applies to all work on equipment where unexpected
energisation could cause harm, including:

- Hydraulic test benches HTB-1 through HTB-6
- The 400-tonne housing press
- CNC machining centres
- Any circuit holding residual pressure above 10 bar

## 3. Authorised Persons

Only a **certified LOTO operator** may apply or remove a lock.
Certification is issued by the Safety Officer after classroom and
practical assessment, and is **valid for 24 months**. Annual refresher
training is mandatory in the intervening year.

A register of certified operators is maintained by HSE and posted at each
maintenance station.

## 4. Lock Colour Convention

| Lock colour | Applied by |
|---|---|
| Red | Mechanical maintenance |
| Yellow | Electrical maintenance |
| Blue | Approved contractor |
| Green | Equipment out of service, long term |

Under no circumstances may a person remove a lock of a colour not
assigned to their discipline.

## 5. The Six-Step Sequence

1. **Notify** — inform the area supervisor and all affected operators of
   the planned isolation and expected duration.
2. **Shut down** — stop the equipment using its normal controls.
3. **Isolate** — operate all energy-isolating devices, including
   electrical disconnects, hydraulic supply valves and pneumatic
   isolators.
4. **Lock and tag** — apply a personal padlock and a completed danger tag
   to each isolation point. The tag must show the operator's name, date
   and work order number.
5. **Dissipate stored energy** — bleed accumulators to zero, relieve line
   pressure, block or lower suspended parts, and allow thermal cooling
   where relevant.
6. **Verify zero energy** — attempt to start the equipment using normal
   controls and confirm gauges read zero before beginning work.

Steps 5 and 6 are the two most commonly skipped and are the subject of
mandatory spot audits.

## 6. Group Lockout

Where more than one person works on the same equipment, a group lockout
box is used. Each worker applies a personal lock to the box hasp. The
equipment may not be re-energised until every personal lock has been
removed by its owner.

## 7. Shift Handover

Locks are not transferred between individuals. An incoming shift applies
its own locks before the outgoing shift removes theirs, so isolation is
never broken.

## 8. Removal of a Lock by Another Person

A lock may be removed by someone other than its owner only with the
written authorisation of the Safety Officer, after documented attempts to
contact the owner and a physical inspection confirming no one is exposed.
The event is recorded as a near-miss.

## 9. Incident Reporting

Any LOTO failure, bypass or near-miss must be reported to the Safety
Officer **within 4 hours**. A formal investigation is opened within 2
working days.

## 10. Records

Completed LOTO permits are retained for 3 years. Audit findings are
tabled at the monthly HSE committee meeting.
